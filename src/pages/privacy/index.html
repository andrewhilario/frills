Privacy

Frills has no accounts. It does keep a few things you choose to send it, such as images and sounds you upload for alerts. Here is exactly what.

What stays on your device

The editor saves your settings and your last channel name in your browser’s local storage, so it remembers you next time. They never leave your device. Clear your browser data and they are gone.

Frills also remembers, in the same way, that you closed the update notice or the small “New here?” suggestion to try the Quick start, and how many times that suggestion has appeared, so it does not keep asking. The Quick start itself saves nothing: your answers stay on its page.

What is in your link

Your overlay link carries your look settings and your channel name. That is how the overlay page knows what to draw. Anyone you share the link with can see them.

How chat is read

On Twitch, your browser or your streaming app connects straight to Twitch’s chat servers to read public chat. Twitch sees a connection from your address, the same as it does for any viewer.

Hosting, cookies and counting

Frills is hosted on Cloudflare, which, like any host, sees ordinary web request data such as your address and the pages you open. Frills itself sets no cookies.

Frills does count, anonymously, so that we can see what is used and what is not. It counts how many visits there are, which page each started on and, in broad groups, where it came from (TikTok, Reddit, Discord, YouTube, a search engine, a link in a profile or an ad, or direct); how many times an overlay link is copied, from which editor; and how many times each overlay page opens, on a real account or on practice events. A count is only a name and one or two short labels from a fixed list. It never holds your address, a cookie or anything you typed.

For an overlay opened on a real account we also keep a scrambled code of the account name, made with a secret that is not in the page and that cannot be turned back into a name. It lets us count how many different overlays open in a week and how many of them come back, and nothing else. Cloudflare keeps the counts for three months. If your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control, the pages count nothing, and neither does an overlay opened with it.

Who we are not
