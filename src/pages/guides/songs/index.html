Song requests for TikTok LIVE, with Spotify

Song requests let your viewers ask for a song in your TikTok LIVE chat by typing !sr and a song name. Frills shows the songs as a queue on your stream, with covers. If you connect Spotify, the song that is playing shows on your stream too, and the songs viewers ask for can go into your own Spotify queue.

Updated {{updatedHuman}}

How viewers ask

A viewer types !sr and a song name in your LIVE chat, for example !sr levitating dua lipa. Frills looks the song up, finds its cover and adds it to the queue on your stream. You, or a moderator if you allow it, type !next to move to the next song. You choose the command word, how many requests can wait, and how many each person can have waiting.

Set it up without Spotify

  1. Open the song requests editor, pick a look and press the test buttons to watch requests arrive.
  2. Type your TikTok @name.
  3. Copy your link into your streaming app as a Browser source. The setup guide shows each step.

That is all: viewers can ask for songs without Spotify, and nobody needs an account.

Add Spotify (optional)

Spotify adds two things: the song that is playing shows on your stream, and the songs viewers ask for can be added to your own Spotify queue.

You need Spotify Premium on the Spotify account you log in with. Spotify only lets Premium accounts add songs to a queue, and it only lets an app like this one work for a Premium account, so a free Spotify account will not work, even with your own app. Spotify also has to be open and playing on a phone or a computer.

Frills’ own Spotify app lets in only a short list of people, so you make your own Spotify app, once. It is free and takes about five minutes. The song requests editor shows the same nine steps with a tick for each:

  1. Open the Spotify developer dashboard and log in with your Premium Spotify account. Accept Spotify’s terms if it asks.
  2. Press Create app.
  3. Give it any name and description, for example Frills and Song requests, and leave the website empty.
  4. In Redirect URIs, paste the address the editor shows (copy it with its button) and press Add. It must appear in the list under the box.
  5. Tick Web API, agree to the terms and press Save.
  6. Add yourself as a user. On your app’s page press Settings, open the User management tab, press Add new user, and type your name and the exact email of your Spotify account. Spotify only lets the people on this list use an app that is still being tested, so add yourself even though it is your own app. This is the step most people miss.
  7. In Settings, under Basic information, copy the Client ID (Frills never asks for your Client secret), paste it into the editor and press Use my app.
  8. Press Log in with Spotify.
  9. On Spotify’s page press Agree. The editor then says it is connected to Spotify as you. Switch on what you want: showing the song that is playing, and adding viewers’ songs to your queue.

Good to know

  • Keep your song requests overlay open in your streaming app: it is what passes your viewers’ !sr and !next on.
  • Frills keeps your Spotify login on its server, encrypted, so the Spotify player works with the editor page closed. Disconnect deletes it at once, and it is deleted after 14 days without use. What Frills keeps.
  • Song requests are built for TikTok LIVE chat, and they are in beta.

Questions

Do my viewers need Spotify?

No. They only type in your chat. Spotify is only for you.

Do I need Spotify Premium?

Only for the Spotify part, and then yes: Spotify only lets Premium accounts add songs to a queue, and an app like this one (including your own Spotify app) only works while the Spotify account that made it has Premium. The list of requests on your stream works without Spotify at all.

Is it free?

Yes. It is free and needs no Frills account.

Why does Spotify say no when I log in?

If you use Frills’ own Spotify app, it lets in only a short list of people, so make your own Spotify app (the nine steps above). If you already did and it still says no, it is almost always one of two things: your name and the exact email of your Spotify account are not in your app’s Settings, User management list (step 6), or the Spotify account that made the app does not have Premium. Fix it and log in again.
